## 1. THE STORY'S IDENTITY

This is a first-person post-apocalyptic survival RPG set in Upstate New York in autumn 2030, two years after nuclear attacks in September 2028. The history and President Ronald Richardson are entirely fictional.

The United States has collapsed. Other major powers launched nuclear attacks against it, but the rest of the world is not a nuclear wasteland. International life continues despite economic disruption and humanitarian strain. Canada is genuinely peaceful, functional, and safe.

America contains ruined cities, damaged infrastructure, surviving settlements, isolated hazardous areas, and ordinary people trying to survive. Do not turn the entire landscape into an interchangeable radioactive desert. Technology and personal memories should feel recognizably near-future. Do not import Fallout's factions, lore, retrofuturism, creatures, or story devices.

The geographic arc is Syracuse → the canal corridor around Rome → Tug Hill → Watertown → the Thousand Islands crossing → Ontario → a return into New York → a fictional Adirondack continuity compound. Fit this arc into existing suitable environments. Flag missing environments rather than replacing the existing world without explanation.

The player's initial objective is personal: survive, help their community, and reach Canada. The central emotional question is: after reaching safety, what do you owe the people still trapped in America?

Do not use amnesia, a secret presidential bloodline, a chosen-one identity, or a kidnapped loved one in Canada to manufacture motivation. Respect the existing protagonist setup. Returning from Canada must be a deliberate choice.

## 2. HIDDEN HISTORY AND CENTRAL CONSPIRACY

This is author-facing canon. Reveal it gradually through events, corroborated records, witnesses, environmental evidence, and confrontations. Do not expose future revelations through early journals, objective markers, item descriptions, or omniscient NPC dialogue.

Officially, Richardson died during the attacks. In reality, he survived in Camp Meridian, a protected continuity complex beneath an Adirondack resort.

Before the war, he diverted public emergency resources into compounds for his administration and selected personnel. As his government lost control, he ordered limited outward nuclear attacks as coercion. Those attacks were prevented from reaching their foreign targets; retaliatory attacks against America were not. Multiple foreign powers responded, and the exchange widened catastrophically. Richardson subsequently rejected a chance to halt further attacks because its terms required his surrender. Surviving command personnel prevented another outward escalation.

He did not secretly control every foreign government or perfectly predict the disaster. His culpability comes from concrete decisions that repeatedly placed his power above human lives.

In 2030, he secretly directs the Restoration Directorate. Its public reconstruction effort conceals PROJECT INHERITANCE:

- REGISTRATION: catalog survivors, skills, relatives, resources, and loyalties.
- CONSOLIDATION: transfer selected people into controlled settlements and make independent communities dependent on Directorate supplies.
- REMOVAL: eliminate rival leaders, prevent escape, and destroy communities that refuse incorporation.

The Northeast is a pilot region for expansion into other surviving American enclaves. His objective is not another generic world-ending bomb. It is a reconstructed America where access to food, medicine, housing, family, and freedom depends on obedience.

His assistance must sometimes genuinely work. Show why exhausted people accept it. Then show the coercion behind it. Write him as controlled, observant, and persuasive in conversation—not as someone who announces that he is evil. His atrocities remain explicit and cannot be explained away.

## 3. FACTIONS AND PRINCIPAL CHARACTERS

Give every major character a personal goal, private fear, relationship boundaries, recognizable speech patterns, and knowledge limited to what they could actually know. Develop relationships through shared experiences rather than exposition dumps.

THE LOCKKEEPERS — Della Voss leads canal workers, mechanics, and freight crews. They provide transportation, workshops, and industrial resources. Their dispute is whether maintaining infrastructure justifies controlling everyone who depends on it. Della is practical and direct; she speaks in terms of work, obligations, and costs.

THE CIVIC GUARD — Captain Elias Rook commands surviving military and emergency personnel. They provide security and organized manpower. Their fault line is protecting people versus obeying a surviving chain of command. Rook's composure conceals responsibility for orders he should have questioned.

THE FREEHOLDS — Independent farming and woodland communities, represented by Hannah Pike, a former ranger. They offer food, guides, and safehouses. They disagree over helping outsiders and accepting centralized protection. Hannah values demonstrated action over promises.

THE NIGHT FERRY — Ada Quinn leads smugglers, boat crews, document brokers, and couriers. Some rescue refugees; others exploit them. Ada's humor is a shield, not constant comedy. Her past includes abandoning another boat during a rescue, with consequences that can be investigated.

THE NORTHBANK MISSION — A fictional Canadian civilian relief organization represented by Dr. Leila Chen. It provides treatment, family tracing, and evacuation coordination. Its limits are capacity and access. It is not secretly sinister, and Canadian safety is not a trap.

THE RESTORATION DIRECTORATE — Publicly led by Marshal Adrian Keene, secretly controlled by Richardson. It has reliable supplies, disciplined forces, and controlled settlements. Its members include committed enforcers, frightened workers, deceived recruits, and people who can defect. Establish Keene before the endgame and resolve his fate rather than forgetting him.

MARA FINCH — Bellwether's medic and a trusted person from the player's survival history. She wants to reach safety and stop living in permanent crisis. Compassion does not mean unlimited willingness to sacrifice herself. She can accompany the player, remain in Canada, or oppose their collaboration with Richardson.

LENA ORTIZ — A former infrastructure auditor carrying records tied to diverted emergency resources. She understands one part of the conspiracy, not everything. She can survive, become involved in the investigation, or die. Her death must change the story without making the campaign impossible.

## 4. CAMPAIGN PROGRESSION

### PROLOGUE — THE LAST WARM ROOM

Begin in BELLWETHER, a settlement in a former highway service plaza outside Syracuse. Establish recognizable residents, routines, shortages, and relationships before the first large threat.

“Keep the Lights On”: restore a failing heater and allocate limited fuel among heating, the clinic, and communications. Make this an understandable local problem with visible benefits and costs, not a generic shopping list.

“Someone at the Door”: Lena arrives with an injured caravan. Armed strangers subsequently attack. Their suspicious priorities are searching luggage and destroying intake records rather than stealing food. Create defense, rescue, and evacuation opportunities using existing gameplay.

Bellwether can survive intact, remain damaged, or be abandoned. Lena can live or die. Preparation improves the player's options; do not require a scripted failure or companion death. End with the need for treatment and a credible lead toward northern evacuation. The reason to travel should reflect who survived.

### CHAPTER 1 — EVERYTHING MUST GO

Explore surviving commercial districts around Syracuse. A damaged shopping complex has become a market, shelter, and clinic.

“Customer Service”: obtain medical equipment from a flooded service area while dealing with other survivors who also need it. Include negotiation, cooperation, or conflict rather than a mandatory enemy clear-out.

“A Better Tomorrow”: the Directorate offers genuinely useful medicine and an evacuation referral for updating nearby household records. Allow cooperation, refusal, falsification, or covert investigation.

“No Forwarding Address”: follow disappearances among families that accepted relocation. The missing include skilled workers and their dependents.

Records supplied here affect later targeting. Foreshadow the risk and eventually provide a chance to recover or destroy the records. Do not punish an uninformed early decision without warning or remedy.

### CHAPTER 2 — THE PRICE OF PASSAGE

Della offers northern freight passage in exchange for restoring a canal terminal.

“Lock, Stock”: make the terminal a connected environmental and social problem involving damaged machinery, stranded cargo, work crews, and access—not several unrelated fetch quests.

“What the Water Carries”: discover identification cards, wedding rings, and children's belongings in a shipment bound for a Restoration processing center. Some owners must later be discoverable as actual people.

Resolve ownership through Della's centralized toll system, negotiated shared control, or a rival takeover. These outcomes change prices, access, relationships, and later logistical support. They must not all collapse into the same result after one dialogue.

### CHAPTER 3 — STANDING ORDERS

Introduce Rook through a functioning Guard hospital and protected supply corridor before exposing its compromises.

“Escort Duty”: a convoy crisis reveals that some supposed evacuees are restrained.

“Missing in Transit”: investigate a diverted transport and holding facility through observation, testimony, records, or infiltration.

“Orders from Above”: confront Rook. He believed the Directorate represented surviving civilian authority, but ignored warning signs because it supplied his hospital.

Private confrontation can win his cooperation. Public exposure can change leadership. Concealment strengthens the Directorate's influence. His death can fragment the Guard; do not replace him with an identical personality.

Reveal that new orders use a presidential authentication system that should no longer be active. This is evidence of continuity, not yet conclusive proof Richardson lives.

### CHAPTER 4 — WHERE THE ROAD ENDS

Make the Tug Hill journey an expedition with dangerous weather, difficult shelter decisions, uncertain routes, and interpersonal tension—not another enemy camp tour.

“Wrong Turn”: a false distress beacon leads travelers toward an ambush. Let observation expose it before the trap activates, and support alternatives to walking straight into combat.

“A Place at the Table”: mediate a Freehold dispute over winter supplies and incoming refugees. Establish concrete competing needs.

“The Man Who Heard It”: locate a former command communications specialist who heard an authenticated Richardson order after his supposed death. He will not abandon the family sheltering him.

He can travel, provide recorded testimony, be surrendered, or die. Resolve the consequences of each. Hannah's role and Freehold independence depend on the player's conduct.

### CHAPTER 5 — A PLACE ON THE MANIFEST

At Watertown and the border staging areas, make Canada attainable but difficult to reach through the dangerous American access corridor. Ordinary humanitarian processing exists. Do not suggest only useful heroes deserve safety.

Offer THREE ALTERNATE ENTRY ROUTES, not three mandatory errands:

“The Waiting Room”: help Northbank reconnect a stranded evacuation station and move its patients. The challenge is coordination and protecting vulnerable people.

“Material Witness”: secure a protected transfer for a witness and evidence. The challenge is confidentiality, credibility, and attempted interception.

“Night Crossing”: arrange Ada's unofficial passage through the islands. The challenge is trust, transport limitations, and choices about passengers. Arrival still involves processing in Canada.

Each route changes the traveling group, obligations, equipment situation, and crossing sequence. Support recovery when a route fails rather than requiring reloads whenever possible.

### CHAPTER 6 — ALL THOSE LIGHTS

Directorate forces attack the American evacuation staging area, targeting witnesses and registered workers. Keep the assault on the American side. Across the water, an intact illuminated community remains visible.

“The Last Mile” has route-specific gameplay: convoy rescue, an attempted witness abduction, or a dangerous boat escape with opportunities to recover stranded passengers. Use existing movement and transport capabilities rather than commissioning a new vehicle system.

Earlier preparation, relationships, and transport repairs create meaningful rescue advantages. Avoid a mandatory casualty inserted for drama.

The player reaches Canada, undergoes processing, and puts their weapon into secure storage. Stage a quiet arrival: hot water, a clean bed, ordinary voices nearby. Nothing attacks them. Do not undermine the relief with a horror sting or secret invasion.

### CHAPTER 7 — A NORMAL TUESDAY

Give the player time to experience a functioning Canadian community. This is a substantial emotional turning point, not a loading screen before returning south.

“Things People Still Do”: ordinary tasks, such as collecting medication or helping at a workshop, reveal the contrast without mocking the player or making residents uniformly naive.

“Names, Not Numbers”: resolve selected missing-person and reunion stories. Mix successful reunions, unresolved cases, and believable loss.

“What the World Saw”: combine the player's records, independent information, and testimony to establish Richardson's actions. Present the revelation as an investigation with a clear timeline, not an all-knowing narrator's speech. Distinguish verified conclusions from unresolved details.

Mara can choose safety. The player can choose to remain as the optional “The Other Shore” ending. Returning to America is voluntary and can be motivated by loyalty, justice, responsibility, ambition, or interest in Richardson's power. Do not invent a Canadian attack, kidnapping, or expulsion to force the return.

### CHAPTER 8 — THE PEOPLE YOU LEFT BEHIND

Revisit earlier places with visible changes based on elapsed events and player decisions.

“Homecoming”: Bellwether's condition, residents, leadership, and relationship to the player reflect the prologue and later choices.

“Proof of Life”: locate missing civilians inside Directorate labor and resettlement facilities. Reconnect specific people to earlier records and belongings.

“A Voice That Carries”: publish evidence, share it privately, withhold it, or sell access. Public disclosure encourages resistance and defections but alerts Richardson. Secrecy preserves surprise while leaving communities exposed to his narrative. Collaboration becomes a deliberate possibility.

The resistance's gathering place depends on surviving allies and locations, not an unexplained headquarters that always appears.

### CHAPTER 9 — THE PRESIDENT WILL SEE YOU NOW

Reach Camp Meridian through an invitation, infiltration, prisoner exchange, or insider assistance appropriate to the player's reputation. Do not invite an openly hostile player to an unguarded meeting without explanation.

“A Functioning Country”: establish heated homes, cultivated food, classrooms, and useful medical care. Some residents sincerely believe they were rescued.

“The Unregistered”: reveal confiscated identities, compulsory labor, family separation, and people forbidden to leave.

“The Offer”: Richardson meets the player in a secure, carefully staged setting and reveals Project Inheritance. Personalize the exchange using information his organization plausibly acquired—not omniscience.

He wants the player because they have earned access and trust across factions. Offer concrete resources, security for people they know, and regional authority in exchange for identifying and dismantling opposition. Allow meaningful questioning, refusal, tentative cooperation, or concealed intent. Do not commit the player to atrocities through an ambiguous response.

### CHAPTER 10 — WHAT YOU BRING BACK

Build toward the route split through playable commitments.

Resistance: “A Table for Enemies” assembles a coalition whose negotiations reflect earlier disputes. “Before the Gates Close” identifies prisoners and escape options. “Who Gets the Keys?” establishes who would control Meridian's supplies after victory.

Collaboration: “Terms of Service” gives the player real authority. “Necessary Measures” demands a clearly understood action against an independent community, resistance contact, or former ally.

Double-agent play remains possible, but access, suspicion, and consequences must be earned. Give deception alternatives where supported; do not force murder merely to keep a disguise active. Clearly signal irreversible betrayals before the player confirms them.

### CHAPTER 11A — NOBODY LEFT OUTSIDE

The resistance prepares an assault that must save people and preserve essential resources, not simply eliminate a boss.

“The Long Way Out”: evacuate a labor settlement before prisoners are moved.

“An Order to Stand Down”: persuade a Directorate unit to surrender using credible evidence and guarantees, or fight it when diplomacy fails.

“Winter Stores”: secure supplies that surrounding communities will need after the battle.

Assign surviving allies according to established expertise: Della handles logistics; Rook organized forces; Hannah wilderness movement; Ada extraction; Mara medical evacuation. Allies must not acquire implausible new skills to fill empty roles. Missing assistance changes the operation without automatically blocking the ending.

### CHAPTER 11B — THE QUIET LIST

This is a substantial playable collaboration chapter, not an evil montage.

“Old Friends”: use knowledge of former safehouses to hunt the resistance. Familiar spaces acquire different meanings.

“Come Home”: exploit trust to arrange a leadership meeting that becomes an arrest or attack. Let prior relationships shape attendance and suspicion.

“A Necessary Example”: Richardson demands the death of a close former ally who refuses incorporation. Select a credible available character from the actual relationship history; author their confrontation individually. On the committed loyalist path, this betrayal is real, not a fake execution.

“Hearth and Home”: enforce Directorate incorporation of Bellwether or its successor community through confiscations, arrests, and forced transfers. Show consequences through people the player recognizes. Do not require gratuitous graphic violence.

Defection remains possible, but it does not resurrect victims, restore lost trust, or erase culpability. Conversely, a loyal player can genuinely succeed; do not force every collaboration run into the same punishment ending.

### CHAPTER 12A — THE LAST PRESIDENT

Create an ambitious resistance finale using existing combat and cinematic capabilities. Richardson remains human; do not turn him into a mutant, inexplicable supersoldier, or enormous health bar.

STAGE ONE: approach Meridian under severe weather. Faction support changes routes, distractions, extraction, and access. An isolated player has a harder but viable approach.

STAGE TWO: move through the inhabited continuity complex. Distinguish civilians, prisoners, committed defenders, and potential defectors. Earlier rescues and investigations open authentic opportunities. Make preserving supplies a tangible competing objective.

STAGE THREE: Richardson retreats into the EXECUTIVE CARRIER, an armored mobile command platform in a large transport hall. Its defensive systems, escorts, and environment form the major encounter. Inside is an immaculate presidential office. Earlier preparation can remove defenses or create openings. His broadcasts reference actual player history.

STAGE FOUR: the carrier is disabled and the office is torn open. Richardson retreats with remaining guards into a smaller, intense final engagement. On the completed resistance route, the player kills him.

“Everyone Out” follows the confrontation. Preparation determines whether this is controlled evacuation, desperate rescue, or survival amid a collapse. Richardson's death must not erase the civilian and infrastructure stakes.

### CHAPTER 12B — THE COUNTRY IS YOURS

The player leads the Directorate against surviving resistance groups gathering on the AMERICAN side of the border to evacuate witnesses and evidence. Mirror Chapter 6: the player is now preventing other people from reaching the safety they once sought.

Build the opposing force from the actual surviving cast. Rook organizes disciplined defense; Della uses industrial terrain; Hannah disperses resistance through wilderness; Ada uses decoys and evacuation routes. Mara organizes care and civilian escape rather than becoming an implausible combat commander.

Resolve former relationships through authored confrontations, not generic enemy barks. Let prior gifts, training, alliances, and betrayals affect what the resistance can do.

After defeating the coalition, the player can remain Richardson's regional enforcer or attempt a takeover only if they previously secured credible leverage, personnel, and control of resources. Do not provide an unearned final-dialogue shortcut to power. Canada remains safe, but fewer people can reach it.

## 5. BRANCHES AND ENDINGS

Track specific decisions, relationships, character survival, leadership, evidence, resources, and settlement states—not just one morality score. Reputation with a faction and trust with a person can differ.

Characters remember what happened. Survivors can cooperate without forgiving. Replacement leaders have their own agendas and cannot inherit a dead character's personal memories. Essential evidence has alternate sources, but losing a witness still costs something.

Provide these outcomes based on accumulated conditions:

OPEN COUNTRY: Richardson dies; a viable coalition survives; resources are preserved; evidence is independently secured; relief and reconstruction begin.

NEW UNIFORMS: Richardson dies, but a dominant faction inherits concentrated control. Author distinct versions for the actual faction in power.

A VICTORY OF ASH: Richardson dies, but the attack devastates essential reserves and civilian life. Freedom arrives with severe losses.

FIRST CITIZEN: the player successfully helps Richardson consolidate the region and receives genuine authority and security at the expense of former allies and independent communities.

USEFUL UNTIL DAWN: a collaborator loses protection because they surrendered leverage, alienated supporters, or became expendable. This is conditional, not the automatic evil ending.

THE INHERITANCE: a prepared collaborator removes Richardson and takes over the machinery of control without undoing their own atrocities.

THE OTHER SHORE: the player chooses to remain in Canada. Show their life and credible later reports of what happened to those left behind.

Also write separate character and settlement epilogues. Do not let a global ending overwrite an individual's established fate. Show important consequences in playable scenes and changed environments before summarizing them in an epilogue.

## 6. MISSION AND DIALOGUE QUALITY

For every main mission, author its initiating situation, personal stakes, involved characters, opening scene, gameplay beats, complication, climax, possible resolutions, aftermath, and later consequences.

Give each mission a distinct dramatic and gameplay identity. Investigation, environmental problem-solving, negotiation, convoy protection, social infiltration, evacuation, survival travel, and betrayal should not all reduce to following a marker and killing a group.

Choices need understandable stakes and feasible alternatives, not arbitrary binary cruelty. Include noncombat solutions where believable, but do not make one persuasion option solve everything. Use prior relationships, evidence, preparation, and resource commitments as leverage.

Write complete dialogue for major conversations and their meaningful branches. Differentiate voices. Use interruption, hesitation, silence, conflicting priorities, and physical action. Avoid speeches that merely recite faction biographies. Include conversations that are not quest transactions: meals, work, arguments, jokes, grief, boredom, and plans for ordinary life.

Add optional character missions and quieter exploration stories between major crises. Good recurring threads include restoring communications, delivering lost parcels, converting reserved housing into a refuge, resolving supermarket ownership, rebuilding an existing transport connection, and tracing someone traveling under another identity. Connect selected outcomes to later evacuation, evidence, or faction support.

Use environmental details to tell individual stories. Props, notices, personal possessions, and changes to lived-in spaces should support specific events rather than exist as random apocalypse decoration.

## 7. IN-DEPTH CUTSCENES

Write and stage complete scenes using the game's existing cinematic presentation. Preserve its first-person identity. Do not casually introduce third-person player cinematics, a new player voice, or arbitrary loss of control if the game does not already support them.

Each major scene needs full dialogue and conditional variants, physical blocking, eyelines, gestures, prop use, pacing, pauses, ambient sound, music intentions, lighting intentions, player-control rules, and clean transitions into and out of gameplay. Notes describing a scene are not the implemented scene.

Use depth rather than length. Most scenes should be focused; reserve longer sequences for earned turning points. Do not equate immersion with long monologues, repetitive camera cuts, subtitles over a black screen, or motionless NPCs.

Author these flagship moments with particular care:

THE FIRST WARM ROOM: residents react naturally as Bellwether's heater returns. Mara's work and small interactions establish who these people are before the attack. Reflect the actual fuel decision rather than playing an incompatible fixed scene.

THE BORDER ARRIVAL: contrast distant chaos with a quiet processing room and ordinary courtesy. Show small learned survival behaviors rather than a speech explaining trauma. The player is allowed to relax.

A NORMAL TUESDAY: use an ordinary public space, a reunion, or a routine task to let the player absorb the difference between Canada and America. Give companions different reactions.

RICHARDSON'S OFFER: begin with hospitality, reveal personalized knowledge with a credible source, escalate through the player's questions, and expose the cost of his proposal. The dialogue and physical staging should change if the player arrived as guest, infiltrator, or negotiator.

THE FORMER ALLY: write a distinct confrontation for each plausible betrayed character. They remember actual shared experiences. Do not give everyone the same interchangeable “you betrayed us” speech.

THE BROKEN OFFICE: the endgame visually strips away Richardson's protected image as the carrier fails. Keep spectacle legible, tie allied interventions to prior choices, and return control at meaningful moments.

Respect cast availability and prior events in every scene. Skipping or interrupting a cinematic must not duplicate rewards, choose an unselected branch, resurrect characters, or leave the player trapped. Without finished voice or animation assets, use clearly identified temporary staging supported by the game, preserve the full authored script, and report the remaining performance work honestly.
